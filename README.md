# California Education Outcomes Analysis

## Overview
This project analyzes California public education data to examine relationships between socioeconomic status, demographics, teacher salaries, and educational outcomes. Using R, multiple statewide datasets were cleaned, integrated, and analyzed to identify patterns that can inform education policy and resource allocation.

## Research Objectives
- Examine relationships between socioeconomic factors and student outcomes.
- Analyze demographic disparities across California school districts.
- Investigate trends in graduation rates and teacher salaries.
- Provide data-driven recommendations for education policy and resource allocation.

## Methodology
- Integrated multiple California public datasets in R.
- Aggregated school-level data into district-level metrics using weighted averages.
- Cleaned and validated datasets by addressing missing values and standardizing variables.
- Conducted statistical analysis and created visualizations to evaluate relationships between key variables.

## Key Findings
- Lower-income counties exhibited substantially higher Free/Reduced-Price Lunch (FRPL) eligibility.
- Schools with larger Hispanic and Black student populations generally showed higher FRPL eligibility.
- Graduation rates varied across counties and geographic regions despite overall statewide improvements.
- The analysis highlighted socioeconomic and demographic disparities that may inform targeted education policies and resource allocation.
